### Section 1 — Baseline, deck repair, turn planning

- First agent: weighted option scorer (values to attack, pass, search, draw, evolution, energy attachment, other legal actions).
- Beat random agent 66.0% over 50 games. Against real public agents won 1/160, Wilson-95 lower bound 0.001.
- Seat effect: 15.0% from first seat, 6.2% from second.
- Loss analysis: 55% of deck was basic energy. Cutting energy 33→13 and adding search raised WR 0.6%→9.2% (22/240), policy unchanged.
- Four weight adjustments later confirmed 13.2% adjusted WR over 520 games.
- Turn-level planning (coordinating attack and energy as one plan) → 20.0% adjusted WR, Wilson lower 0.160.
- Combat probe matched engine's damage result in all 16 legal-attack cases tested. Dragapult gap was NOT damage arithmetic — it was a multi-turn prize race.

### Section 2 — Local rules, imitation, shallow search

| Method | Result | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| One energy-acceleration rule | 25.0% vs 32.5% diagnostic baseline | Local rule hurt |
| Ten-line additive playbook | 35.0% vs 26.2%, but under +10pp gate | Some signal, no promotion |
| High-authority overrides | 26.2%→21.2%→20.0% | More forcing made policy worse |
| Free-standing imitation | 33.8%, 33.8%, 36.2%, 32.5% over 4 rounds | Stalled below parity |
| One-ply engine search | 30.0% mirror WR | Worse than best imitation round |

- Free-standing pilot disagreed with expert on <8% of logged decisions. Did not translate to on-policy strength. Small errors sent policy into states the teacher rarely reached.
- Local overrides failed: fixing one move could damage rest of turn (e.g. preventing a bad retreat by ending development).

### Section 3 — Reinforcement learning

- Small value network from self-play: clearest original ML gain.
- First loop oscillated. Added all-history replay buffer + slow target network → mirror results 42.0%, 39.5%, 45.5%, 40.5%, 49.0%, 54.5%. Final Wilson lower 47.6%.
- Gain did NOT generalize:

| Experiment | Result |
|---|---|
| Mirror-trained value net on 3 non-mirror opponents | Wilson lower 0.1762 |
| Four-deck league training | Wilson lower 0.1575 |
| Card-aware reactive policy | Wilson lower 0.1653, adj WR 0.195 |
| Teacherless A2C | Wilson lower 0.1777, adj WR 0.2083 |
| A2C with replay and target critic | Wilson lower 0.1793, adj WR 0.210 |

- Deck-policy entanglement severe: Iono reference pilot competent, but card-net on same deck fell to Wilson lower 0.0285.
- DAgger: raised single-select agreement 0.61→0.74, non-mirror WR stayed 0.167. Three full-decision training regimes: 0.183, 0.217, 0.208. A stateful hand-coded heuristic scored 0.743, Wilson lower 0.707, over 600 games.
- Stopped investing in stateless per-option scoring nets under this representation. Budget decision, not general claim.

### Section 4 — Generalists, specialists, deck variants

- Generalist controller tempo problem: vs 9,097 top-agent decisions, agreement 84.7% on playing cards from hand, but 21.2% on energy attachment, 19.4% on evolution, 27.5% on attack. Re-prioritizing raised pooled WR 0.305→0.380. Card-aware targeting improved off-policy agreement but reduced on-policy WR 0.380→0.358.
- Deck-specific policies did better:

| Pilot | Result |
|---|---|
| Dragapult specialist | 0.645 field WR, Wilson lower 0.588 |
| Deck-agnostic policies on same task | 0.138 |
| Lucario specialist | 0.500 field WR vs 0.165 incumbent |
| Water specialist | 0.490 mirror, 0.475 field |
| Durant mill specialist | 0.525 mirror vs 0.145 generalist |
| Durant mill field result | 0.110 |

- Mill specialist cut self-deck-out 64%→33%, doubled deck pressure, but deck remained weak vs field.
- Lucario localizer: zero disagreements vs source across 4,954 decisions → residual mirror loss not a missing single-pick rule.
- Opening-hand simulation: 50,000-draw model found water variant cutting mulligans 0.456→0.259. Mirror result fell 0.525→0.345. Better setup ≠ better play.

### Section 5 — Measurement and packaging

- **Common Random Numbers (CRN)**: interposed on native shuffle so two policies play same deals. Identical policies → identical trajectories. Variance reduction ~4.88x for near-clones, ~1.18x for dissimilar. Must be measured per comparison.
- **Perturbation control**: replay rescue initially looked useful. Random-choice control rescued at same rate:
  - Random first-K legal choices: 0.368 rescue
  - Oracle bounded arm: 0.316
  - LLM bounded arm: 0.316
  - Old comparison used unchanged replay as floor (~0 by construction). After control, rescue claims had to beat a perturbation-matched floor.
- **Packaging**: Kaggle executed agent in bare namespace where `__file__` was undefined → agents using it at module load crashed before playing. Local tests also hid SDK dependencies.
  - Fix: pure-Python SDK shim, bundled card database, legal fail-closed behavior, isolated smoke test (extract tarball, deny ambient SDK, load in bare namespace, force decisions). Next canary returned COMPLETE.

### Section 6 — Offline proxies and the live ladder

- Live rating is an Elo fixed point, not raw win rate:
  `settled ≈ mean_opponent_rating + 400 * log10(p / (1 - p))`
  Approximate because opponent distribution changes as agent climbs. Explains how agent can have losing record and still settle highest after reaching stronger rating band.
- Ladder operational rules: new agents seeded near 600, provisional ratings can overshoot, only the two most recent submissions play ranked games, result needs ~24h to settle.
- Offline weighted-field gate failed as final judge: Dragapult candidate cleared +0.059 CI [+0.014, +0.104] vs Alakazam, but live ordering was Alakazam ~820.5 vs Dragapult ~797.8. Offline pool contained too many of their own pilots.
- Dudunsparce rebuild passed offline gates, settled at 369.3. Added acceleration to reduce time-to-powered-attacker turn 19→~5. Live result: setup speed was not the binding issue.

### Section 8 — Attempts to improve the copied policy

| Attempt | Result |
|---|---|
| Constant tuning | 42 CRN pairs over 6 candidates; no 95% CI cleared zero |
| Immediate active-KO finish mode | Delta exactly zero over 73 paired games |
| Finish mode activation | ~23.8% of games, but only reordered actions |
| Shared execution layer | Changed top choice on 0.83% of decisions |
| High-field loss patching | Misplays existed but scattered outside ceiling matchups |
| Alakazam re-fire | Returned to ~803.6 |

- High-field analysis: wins and losses both assembled by turn 4, powered by turn 5. Wins ended ~turn 11. Losses vs grinding decks extended to turns 17-19, where Alakazam's one-energy damage clock was outpaced.
- Did NOT run full RL fine-tuning from Alakazam copy. Remains unmeasured. RL needed cheap reward aligned with ladder strength + runnable versions of ceiling opponents. Had neither. Self-play already produced 54.5% mirror + weak field transfer.

### Section 9 — Signals I would not trust again

| Signal | What invalidated/limited it |
|---|---|
| Win rate vs random | 66.0% → 0.6% vs real |
| A locally correct rule | Energy acceleration rule lowered diagnostic result |
| Imitation accuracy | <8% disagreement still missed on-policy parity |
| Mirror RL | 54.5% mirror did not transfer to field |
| More opponent variety by itself | Four-deck league did not improve field result |
| Better opening consistency | Lower-mulligan water variant lost mirror |
| Replay rescue without random intervention arm | Random choices rescued as often as teacher |
| An offline weighted field as final judge | Ranked Dragapult over stronger live Alakazam |
| Faster setup as sufficient fix | Dudunsparce acceleration settled 369.3 |
| Local runtime success | Kaggle bare loader broke `__file__` and hidden SDK deps |
| Strategy prose as usable teacher | Ceiling-defining policies not executable |

### Section 10 — Recommendations

1. Use random only for smoke. Benchmark against real public agents early.
2. Seat-swap evaluations and use lower bounds for promotion.
3. Separate mirror and non-mirror gates. Test transfer before investing further.
4. Fix deck structure before adding policy complexity.
5. Add turn coherence before writing many local rules.
6. Treat imitation agreement as a diagnostic, not an outcome metric.
7. Use CRN for near-clone comparisons, and measure its variance reduction.
8. Add a random perturbation arm to replay-rescue tests.
9. Treat offline opponent pools as screens. Let settled live results override them.
10. Reproduce submission runtime in isolation and fail closed with legal actions.
11. Try specialists when a generalist is weak, but do not assume a specialist can rescue weak deck content.
12. Copying a mature public policy can be rational use of limited budget. Call it copying and test whether your changes improve it.

### Final read

Original methods raised 0.6% agent into useful local regimes. Deck repair, turn planning, self-play, specialist control, CRN, perturbation controls, packaging gates all worked for their intended purpose. They did not produce the best live policy.

Strongest sourceable policy: Alakazam copy (~802-803). Local tuning and search patches did not improve it. Top opponents that defined its ceiling were not available as runnable policies — limited training and offline eval.

Durable result: the failure map. Random win rate, local rules, imitation agreement, mirror performance, setup consistency, replay rescue, and offline weighted fields all looked stronger than they were.
