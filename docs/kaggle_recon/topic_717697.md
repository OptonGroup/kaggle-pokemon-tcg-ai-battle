Absolutely! Happy to share.
1-a. You can look up high placing decks here: https://limitlesstcg.com/ (\1) . There's also past tournaments here which have a wildly different metagame than Kaggle https://labs.limitlesstcg.com/ (\1) . Recent tournaments Indianapolis through Prague match the card pool of this competition.

Here's an example: https://www.pokemon.com/us/features/pokemon-tcg-deck-list-and-strategy-building-a-mega-lucario-ex-deck (\1) .
1-c. All of the videos here are good value for time invested https://www.youtube.com/@sixpokemanstcg (\1) . I'd specifically recommend https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jru0f1x7E14 (\1) and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=owUa7ohI7R0 (\1) . The concept of **sequencing **and **prize mapping** are important for almost every deck. 
Since I'm newer I'm not 100% confident I use the phrases 100% correct but I'm more correct than I'm not. ;-]
**Sequencing **is knowing the right actions to take to have the most information available. Here's a very common example - you have Fezandipiti Ex (Fez) and Drakloak and Dreepy in play, and you have a Poke Pad (Pad) in hand. Let's say your goal is to find Stage 1 Drakloak, Basic Dreepy, and Basic Duskull on your turn. The less optimal sequencing is to use Poke Pad first for one of your 3 targets, then using Fez's Flip The Scripts (Draw3). For example, maybe I poke pad for Drakloak so I guarantee have 2 Drakloak abilities to use in order to see more cards, but there's a chance I find more Drakloak in my Fez's Draw 3 afterwards. Instead, if we use the generic draw of Fez first, we have the opportunity to see any of our 3 finds - if we found an item Buddy-Buddy Poffin (Poffin) off of Fez draw, we now are guaranteed to find those 3 cards we want with Poffin and Poke Pad! At this point, it could be optimal to use the Poffin and Pad now: I need to ask myself, "With the cards I now have, if I use Drakloak's ability and see a Dreepy (replace Dreepy as many times as needed with other cards).. would I have wished I saw different options?" and then decide to 'thin' the deck of cards I didn't want to see with other cards in my hand, in order to improve my chances of seeing other cards. In this example I've described, I may prefer to use my Poffin and Pad before using my 2 Drakloak's abilities, because I perceive higher value in seeing other cards that I cannot specifically search for at this point (from Magic the Gathering, searching your deck for a specific card is called 'tutor' or 'tutoring' so you may see that in PTCG too). So, the basic lesson is **generic draw first, then search for specific cards**. The primary exception to this rule is if you guarantee know what you need to see in your deck, you will "**thin to win**" aka discard your deck in order to have a better chance to randomly draw a card you're seeking.
**Prize mapping** is basically asking, "How will I take knockouts in order to get 6 prizes this game?" People will say a series of numbers like 2-2-2 meaning "I will take 3 knockouts across 3 turns in a way that gets me two prizes (usually a 2-prize pokemon and not 2 single prize pokemon, but it depends) at a time." There's a lot of minutiae / things to focus in on around prize mapping.. usually matchup dependent. It ties in a lot with "**prize trading / prize racing**". Prize trading is simply knowing how many prizes you intend to give up as a result of your actions. A good trade can be taking a 2-prize knockout on Fez and losing a 1-prize Alakazam in return. This prize trading builds into and augments your prize race against your opponent. Your prize map may look like 2-2-2, and your opponent on the other end will be looking to disrupt your prize map. For example if you only need 2 prizes left to win and your opponent's board is single prizers plus one Meowth Ex, they could play Scoop Up Cyclone on Meowth to change into a single prize board state, now forcing you to play for one more turn.
Some decks strategize to 'make your opponent take 7 or 8 prizes' by forcing your opponent into a 1-2-2 prize map, and then benching a 3-prizer (a mega pokemon in this format) afterwards, ensuring your opponent has to work harder to reach their goal of passing 6 prizes. Legacy Energy is good to disrupt a prize map. Having cards like Boss's Orders let's you maintain your prize map in case your targets are not being pushed to the active. Some decks can maintain a 1 prize board state for a long time Grimmsnarl Froslass is good at this) until they want to evolve into their 2 prize pokemon.
